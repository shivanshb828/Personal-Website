---

Attention is the only truly scarce resource. Not time — we all have the same twenty-four hours. Not even energy, which can be recovered with sleep. Attention is different. Once spent on something unworthy, it's simply gone.

I've been more deliberate lately about what I give my attention to. Not in a productivity-obsessive way, but in the quieter sense of asking: *is this worth thinking about?*

---

A few things I've noticed:

The cost of context-switching is much higher than it appears. Each switch doesn't just cost the time of the switch — it costs the depth you'd accumulated. You have to rebuild.

Notifications are not neutral. Every notification is a small argument that what the sender wants is more important than what you were doing. Most of the time, they're wrong.

Boredom is increasingly rare and increasingly valuable. The moments when there's nothing to look at, nothing to respond to — those moments, if you stay in them, tend to produce something. A thought. A question. A connection. The tendency to fill these moments is, I think, a loss.

---

I'm not sure what to do with these observations besides act on them slowly. Turn off more things. Protect more gaps. Let more conversations be slow.

The internet has made instantaneity the default. Everything happens in real time, at the speed of the fastest participant. I find myself wanting to opt out of that rhythm more and more.
